Why I Write This

I've noticed a pattern across every company I've worked with: most product marketing is built on assumptions, not insights.

We assume we know what customers care about. We assume we know how to position our product. We assume our messaging will resonate. And then we're surprised when it doesn't.

The companies that succeeded weren't the ones with the best product. They were the ones that understood their customers deeply and translated that understanding into clear, compelling positioning.

"Go-to-market isn't about marketing tricks. It's about understanding what your customer actually cares about and communicating it in a way that moves them to action."

This site is where I share frameworks, case studies, and hard-won lessons from actually shipping products in regulated, competitive, complex markets.

What I Actually Care About

  • Clarity over cleverness. A clearly-communicated mediocre product beats a vaguely-explained great one.
  • Data over intuition. I use win/loss analysis, customer research, and competitive intelligence to inform strategy.
  • Systems over tasks. Good marketing scales because it's built on frameworks, not individual heroics.
  • Understanding before action. Every campaign starts with: what does the customer actually need to understand?
  • Results over activity. Marketing should move revenue, not just move campaigns.

How I Think

I approach every marketing challenge as a systems problem, not a campaign problem. That means:

  1. Start with the customer. Not the product, not your KPIs. What does the customer actually need to understand?
  2. Build a positioning framework. Don't create messaging in isolation. Anchor it to a clear competitive and value positioning.
  3. Test and iterate. Use win/loss analysis, customer interviews, and messaging testing to validate your assumptions.
  4. Operationalize it. Turn your thinking into sales collateral, email campaigns, content, and enablement materials.
  5. Measure and refine. Track what works. Double down on what resonates. Kill what doesn't.
